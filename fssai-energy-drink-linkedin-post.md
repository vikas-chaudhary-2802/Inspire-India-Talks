# FSSAI Energy-Drink Order — LinkedIn Post

## Caption

**India just told Red Bull, Sting and Monster they can't call themselves "energy drinks" anymore.**

In a quiet but significant move, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has directed beverage companies to drop the term "energy drink" from their labels — and to remove health claims like *"vitalises body and mind," "combats fatigue,"* and *"removes weakness."* Companies have **90 days** to comply.

The reason is simple, and revealing: under Indian food law, there is **no defined category called an "energy drink."** These products are legally just **caffeinated beverages.** Marketing them as something more — with therapeutic-sounding promises that have no statutory backing — amounts to misbranding.

Six major brands have been served notice:
→ Red Bull
→ Sting (PepsiCo)
→ Adrenaline Rush (PepsiCo)
→ Campa Energy – Gold Boost (Reliance)
→ Hell Energy
→ Monster Energy (Coca-Cola-backed)

One important nuance: **this is not a ban.** The drinks stay on shelves — but they must be sold and labelled as caffeinated beverages, with proper caffeine limits and warnings, not as wellness products.

Some states aren't waiting. **Rajasthan** has already seized over **5 lakh cans**, including 53,000+ Red Bull bottles from a single Jaipur warehouse, and told Amazon, Flipkart, Blinkit and Swiggy Instamart to stop promoting these products as "energy drinks."

It's a reminder that in India's consumer market, the fastest-growing categories are increasingly the ones the regulator looks at hardest — and that the story a label tells has to match what's actually in the can.

Would you rethink these products if they were simply labelled "high-caffeine drinks"?
