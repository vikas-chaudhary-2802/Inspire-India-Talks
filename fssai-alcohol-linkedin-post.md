# FSSAI Liquor Crackdown — LinkedIn Post

## Caption

**After energy drinks, India's food regulator has turned its attention to your whisky and rum.**

Fresh off ordering beverage firms to drop the "energy drink" label, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) has widened its crackdown to **alcoholic beverages** — this time over what's actually inside the bottle, and what the label claims about it.

The regulator has issued notices to manufacturing units of **United Spirits Ltd (USL)** — owned by Diageo — and **INBREW Beverages**, alleging the use of **added flavours that mimic a product's natural profile.** In plain terms: lab testing reportedly found artificial or nature-identical flavours "masking" the natural character of the spirit, making the product substandard. In one case, a **"7 years old blended"** claim was flagged because matured spirit allegedly made up **less than 5%** of the blend — the rest being neutral, unaged spirit.

Products and units named include:
→ McDowell's No. 1 Rum (USL, Baramati)
→ Antiquity Blue Whisky & Royal Challenge Whisky (USL, Madhya Pradesh)
→ Facilities of Mohan Rocky Springwater, INBREW Beverages and Associated Alcohol & Breweries

The alleged violations centre on three things: **added flavours, age-related claims, and blend-age disclosures.**

This sits alongside India's broader 2026 alcohol-labelling reforms, which now make it **mandatory** to declare the number of **standard drinks** on the pack (one standard drink = 10g of pure alcohol, per WHO) and to flag allergens like egg white or isinglass used in processing.

The industry is pushing back. **United Spirits has filed a writ petition in the Bombay High Court**, arguing its label declarations comply with existing law and long-standing industry practice.

The bigger picture: from honey and ghee to energy drinks and now premium spirits, FSSAI's message is consistent — **a label has to tell the truth about what's in the product.** For India's consumer brands, "how we've always done it" is no longer a defence.

Should age statements and "natural flavour" claims on alcohol be held to the same standard as any other food label?
