# FSSAI Liquor Sale-Ban — LinkedIn Post

## Caption

**FSSAI has just banned the sale of six popular whisky and rum variants — including Old Monk and McDowell's No. 1.**

On **August 3, 2026**, India's food regulator, the FSSAI, ordered a halt to the sale of select variants from some of the country's best-known liquor brands. Lab testing found these products were built using **externally added artificial or "nature-identical" flavours** to mimic the aroma and taste of properly aged rum and whisky — while being marketed as standard, matured spirits. In at least one case, a **"7-year" age claim** sat on a blend made largely from neutral spirit.

The variants barred from sale:
→ **Old Monk** — The Legend, Gold Reserve & XXX Matured Rum
→ **Bagpiper** — Deluxe Whisky
→ **Old Cask** — Deluxe XXX Rum
→ **Antiquity Blue** — Whisky
→ **Royal Challenge** — Whisky
→ **McDowell's No. 1** — Rum (select variant)

*(Produced at United Spirits and Mohan Rocky Springwater facilities.)*

**Three things worth knowing:**
1. This is **not a full brand ban.** Only these specific variants, made at specific plants, are affected — a regular compliant bottle of the same brand isn't covered.
2. It's a **sale prohibition**, not a safety recall. The issue is honesty on the label — how the flavour and age were represented — not contamination. Bottles already with consumers are not affected.
3. It's being **contested.** United Spirits (Diageo) has challenged the order in the Bombay High Court, so the final outcome isn't settled.

The signal to India's consumer industry is unmistakable: whether it's honey, energy drinks or premium whisky, the regulator now expects the label to tell the truth about what's really inside.

Would a "7-year aged" tag change your buying decision — and should it have to be earned?
